Who Origin Is Built For: The Indie Operator

So who is Origin actually built for? The operator I have in mind is the indie one: freelance designers, coaches, photographers, and consultants, the kind of people who run a real business of one. Line them up and a clear pattern emerges. They send five to twenty contracts a month, which is enough volume to feel the pain but not enough to justify an enterprise tool. Many of them are on DocuSign or something like it, paying for far more product than they could ever use, and they feel that bill arrive every single month. My hope is that most of them find CyberSygn through the blog or a mention somewhere rather than an ad, read a post about field detection or pricing, click through, and try it on a real contract. Here is the part I want to be honest about. The Origin discount is a small lever, and while early adopter pricing helps at the margin, it is not what will make anyone stay, because three dollars off does not change anyone's life. The far bigger lever is detection. The first time fields appear on their own, the value lands, and that is the precise moment a curious visitor turns into a paying customer. Real indie SaaS milestones get built on the product working as promised, not on the coupon, and that is the bet Origin is placing.

What Origin Is Built Around: The Contracts I Am Designing For

Now for the part that shapes the roadmap: the kinds of contracts I am building CyberSygn around. I do not have a hundred members' worth of data to report yet, and I am not going to invent it. What I can share is the hypothesis the product is built on, and what the first real customers will help me confirm or correct. The contract types I am designing for tell a tight, consistent story: NDAs, freelance scopes of work, coaching agreements, and photographer model releases. See the thread running through them? These are all small B2B contracts sent by independent operators, short documents dispatched frequently by one person who wears every hat in the business. That pattern is the beachhead, the specific group a product is built to win first before it expands wider, and it sets the roadmap. As real usage comes in, whichever contract types lead the pack will tell me which templates to build, which fields to tune, and which workflows to smooth out next. The first hundred will not merely be customers, they will be the clearest signal I have about what to build next, which is exactly why the Origin wall is public. There is one more thing this crowd tends to do. These operators send the same kinds of contracts over and over again, so a coach signs the same coaching agreement with each new client, and a photographer sends the same model release at every shoot. That repetition is a genuine gift, because it means templates and saved fields pay off quickly for exactly this crowd. So the plan for the first hundred is simple: let them tell me who CyberSygn is for and what to build for them. That insight will be the real Origin members progress, worth far more than the revenue it brings in today.
