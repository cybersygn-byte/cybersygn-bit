# CyberSygn Ambassador Academy

Tracks 1-3. The paid curriculum that turns a signed-up ambassador into someone who actually moves signups. Track 0 (onboarding: get your code, understand the deal, send your first invite) is assumed complete before anyone enters Track 1.

**What you earn is simple.** Every lesson here is built around one honest promise: your job is to put CyberSygn in front of people who sign contracts, and you get paid on what those people actually buy. We never show you projected or estimated earnings. We show you what each sale pays you, and then it is up to how many real people you reach.

**Your three placeholders, every time.** Drop these literally into any message, post, or asset. The app fills them in:
- `{{CODE}}` is your promo code.
- `{{LINK}}` is your invite link, `https://cybersygn.io/?ref={{CODE}}`.
- `{{DISCOUNT}}` is the offer your audience gets, "20% off their first 3 months".

**The one FTC rule you cannot skip.** Anything public (a post, a caption, a comment, a story, a reel) must carry a clear disclosure like "(affiliate, I earn a commission)". A private one-to-one message does not legally need it, but you stay honest there too. When in doubt, disclose.

## Track 1: Lead Generation

Where warm and cold leads for a contract-signing tool actually come from. The core insight of this track: CyberSygn sells itself to anyone who sends the same agreement over and over and hates the friction. Your job is to find those people, not to convince random strangers they need e-signature.

### Lesson 1.1: The "sends contracts weekly" filter

Before you pitch anyone, learn to spot the tell. The best CyberSygn buyer is someone who sends a proposal, an NDA, a service agreement, or an onboarding form more than once a week and currently does it by email, print-and-scan, or an overpriced tool they resent. You are not looking for "everyone," you are looking for repeat signers.

**Action:** List 15 people in your own contacts who you know send contracts or agreements regularly, freelancers, coaches, contractors, landlords, anyone. That list is your warm launch pad.

**Reference:** Dashboard script "Warm List Builder" (prompts you through the 15 with the questions that surface a real need).

### Lesson 1.2: Freelancer communities, the warmest cold pond

Freelancers feel signing friction more than almost anyone, and they gather in findable places: Discord servers, subreddits, Slack groups, Upwork/Contra forums, and niche Facebook groups for designers, writers, editors, and developers. You show up as a helpful member who happens to use a tool that fixed a real annoyance, never as a link-dropper. The move is to answer "how do you all handle contracts?" threads honestly.

**Action:** Join or identify 3 freelancer communities you actually belong in, and read the rules on self-promotion before you post anything.

**Reference:** Creative asset "Community Reply Pack" (5 honest, non-spammy replies to common contract-pain questions, each ending with your `{{LINK}}` and an affiliate disclosure).

### Lesson 1.3: Coaches and agency owners as multipliers

A single freelancer is one signup. A coach with 40 clients or an agency owner with a team is a multiplier, because they will roll a tool out to everyone they touch. These people already tell their audience what tools to use. If CyberSygn saves them time on client agreements, they become an advocate who does your reach for you.

**Action:** Identify 5 coaches or agency owners in your network or niche, and send each the private "multiplier" message (no disclosure needed on a 1:1, but stay honest).

**Reference:** Dashboard script "Multiplier DM" (the private message that frames CyberSygn as something they can hand to their whole roster, with `{{LINK}}` and `{{DISCOUNT}}`).

### Lesson 1.4: Local business owners, the offline lane

Not every lead lives online. Real estate agents, gyms, tutors, contractors, salons booking chairs, and small law or accounting offices all sign paperwork constantly and often still print it. This lane is less crowded because most ambassadors ignore it, which is exactly why it converts. A two-minute in-person or local-group conversation beats a hundred cold DMs.

**Action:** Pick 3 local businesses you already patronize and mention CyberSygn to the owner next time you are there, then follow up with your `{{LINK}}` by text.

**Reference:** Creative asset "Local Owner One-Pager" (a printable, plain-language sheet showing the drop-a-PDF-to-signed flow, with a QR code that resolves to your `{{LINK}}`).

### Lesson 1.5: Following up without being annoying

Most signups are lost in the gap between "that sounds useful" and "I signed up." The fix is a light, honest follow-up rhythm: one helpful nudge a few days later, then let it rest. You are reminding, not pressuring, and the free first document (3 free lifetime, no card) is your best follow-up line because there is zero risk for them to try it.

**Action:** For every warm lead who did not convert this week, send one follow-up that leads with "try your first document free, no card," and then stop.

**Reference:** Dashboard script "Gentle Follow-Up" (a 3-touch sequence that stays warm and ends cleanly, each touch pre-loaded with your `{{LINK}}`).

**BUILD TRIGGER for Track 1:** Build and release Track 1 when 10 or more ambassadors are active, OR when Track 0 completion stalls (more than 30 percent of onboarded ambassadors finish onboarding but never send a first invite). The stall signal means people know the deal but do not know where leads come from, which is exactly the gap this track closes.

## Track 2: Content and Social

How to make short-form content that earns signups instead of scrolls. The whole track rests on one demo that does the selling for you: the "watch it find every field" moment. You do not have to be a good creator. You have to point a camera at a genuinely satisfying three-second thing.

### Lesson 2.1: The hook is the whole game

On short-form video, the first two seconds decide everything. Your hook should name the pain out loud, "I used to spend 20 minutes prepping every contract for signature," so the right person stops scrolling and the wrong person leaves. A hook that filters is better than a hook that flatters.

**Action:** Write 5 different opening lines for your CyberSygn video, each naming a specific contract-signing pain, and read them out loud to hear which lands.

**Reference:** Creative asset "Hook Bank" (30 tested opening lines sorted by audience: freelancers, agencies, local business).

### Lesson 2.2: The "watch it find every field" demo

This is your money shot. You drop a PDF, and on screen the viewer watches AI locate every signature, date, and initial field in about three seconds. It is visual, it is fast, and it proves the promise without you saying a word. Film it once, clean, in a screen recording, and you will reuse it forever.

**Action:** Record one clean 10-second screen capture of dropping a real PDF and the fields being detected, no cuts, no fakery.

**Reference:** Creative asset "Field-Detection Demo Kit" (a sample PDF safe to show, plus a shot list and the exact on-screen caption to overlay).

### Lesson 2.3: Before and after, the time-saved story

People buy the gap between their bad day and their better day. Show the "before" (printing, scanning, emailing, chasing, re-sending) against the "after" (drop, detect, send, signed, certificate). Keep it honest: show the real steps CyberSygn removes, never invent a number you cannot stand behind.

**Action:** Film or storyboard one before/after clip using your own real workflow, and state only the time saving you actually experienced.

**Reference:** Creative asset "Before/After Template" (a two-panel edit structure and caption formula that keeps claims honest).

### Lesson 2.4: The certificate as proof, not hype

Every completed document gets a tamper-evident SHA-256 certificate, and almost nobody making content mentions it. That is your differentiator content: a short clip explaining, in plain words, that when the doc is signed you get a receipt that proves it was not altered. Trust is a feature people will screenshot and share.

**Action:** Make one short video that shows a real certificate and explains tamper-evidence in one plain sentence, no jargon.

**Reference:** Dashboard script "Certificate Explainer" (a 40-word plain-language script anyone can read to camera).

### Lesson 2.5: Caption, disclosure, and call to action

The video earns the stop, the caption earns the click. Every public caption needs three things: a one-line restatement of the payoff, a clear affiliate disclosure like "(affiliate, I earn a commission)", and your `{{LINK}}` with the `{{DISCOUNT}}` spelled out. Put the disclosure where it is obviously visible, not buried under "more."

**Action:** Write the full caption for your first post, including disclosure, `{{DISCOUNT}}`, and `{{LINK}}`, and post it.

**Reference:** Creative asset "Caption + Disclosure Pack" (fill-in-the-blank captions per platform with the FTC disclosure already placed correctly).

**BUILD TRIGGER for Track 2:** Build and release Track 2 when at least 5 ambassadors have driven a confirmed signup from Track 1 outreach (proof that the offer converts when a real person delivers it), OR when ambassadors start asking for shareable creative on their own. Do not build content tooling before you know the message converts one-to-one, or you will scale a pitch that does not land.

## Track 3: Objections and Honest Positioning

What to say when someone pushes back, and where the honest line sits. This is the track that protects the brand. An ambassador who overclaims to close one sale costs more trust than they earn. The rule under everything here: it is better to lose a signup than to say something we cannot stand behind.

### Lesson 3.1: "Is it actually legally binding?"

This is the first question serious buyers ask, and you answer it with calm facts, not hype. Electronic signatures are broadly recognized for most everyday business agreements, and every CyberSygn document is backed by a tamper-evident SHA-256 certificate that shows the signed file was not altered. You are honest about the boundary too: you are not a lawyer, and certain documents (some wills, specific notarized filings) have their own rules, so you point people to check their local requirements for anything unusual.

**Action:** Practice saying the binding answer out loud in under 30 seconds, including the "I am not a lawyer, check local rules for unusual documents" line.

**Reference:** Dashboard script "Legally Binding Answer" (the exact honest wording, plus the short list of "check first" document types).

### Lesson 3.2: "How is this different from DocuSign?"

Do not trash DocuSign. Position on the real differences: CyberSygn finds every field with AI in about three seconds so there is no manual field-dragging, signers need no account to sign, and the price is a fraction. The honest frame is "same signed, binding result, far less setup and far less cost," not "DocuSign is bad."

**Action:** Write your own one-paragraph answer to "why not just use DocuSign," leading with the AI field detection and no-account signing.

**Reference:** Creative asset "Difference Card" (a side-by-side of the three real differentiators, worded to compare without insulting).

### Lesson 3.3: Price objections and the free first document

When someone says "I do not want another subscription," you do not discount harder, you lower the risk. The first document is genuinely free (3 free lifetime, no card), so they can prove the value before paying anything, and your `{{DISCOUNT}}` takes 20 percent off their first 3 months when they are ready. Plans start at Solo $12/mo, so the honest line is "it costs less than the time you lose doing it the old way."

**Action:** Rehearse the price response that leads with "try it free first, no card," then names the real starting price of $12/mo.

**Reference:** Dashboard script "Price Objection Handler" (the free-first-then-`{{DISCOUNT}}` flow with the real plan prices: Solo $12, Pro $19, Studio $29, Business $79).

### Lesson 3.4: The never-overclaim line

Your reputation is the asset. Never promise something CyberSygn does not do, never invent a legal guarantee, never quote a time saving you did not experience, and never imply an endorsement that is not real. If you do not know the answer, "I am not sure, let me find out" beats a confident guess every single time. This lesson exists because one overclaim can undo a hundred honest sends.

**Action:** Read the "things we never say" list, then screen your own last three messages or posts against it and fix anything that drifts.

**Reference:** Dashboard script "Honest Positioning Checklist" (the never-say list plus the approved way to phrase each claim).

### Lesson 3.5: Matching the plan to the person

Recommending the wrong plan feels like a sale but creates a refund and a bad review. Learn the quick fit: solo freelancers usually want Solo $12 or Pro $19 if they want the AI drafting co-pilot, small teams want Studio $29 for 3 seats, and higher-volume operations want Business $79. When you match honestly, the signup sticks and the commission holds.

**Action:** For your three warmest current leads, write down which plan actually fits each and why, in one sentence each.

**Reference:** Dashboard script "Plan Fit Guide" (a short decision path from "how many people sign and send" to the right plan, with each plan's real price).

**BUILD TRIGGER for Track 3:** Build and release Track 3 the moment ambassadors start fielding live objections at volume, concretely when 3 or more ambassadors report a "they asked me X and I did not know what to say" moment, OR when any ambassador is caught overclaiming in public. The overclaim signal is urgent: ship this track immediately, because unmanaged objection-handling is where brand trust leaks fastest.
